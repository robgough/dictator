# iOS Changelog

User-facing changes to Dictator for iOS. Internal changes — refactors,
build tweaks, dependency bumps that don't change behaviour — don't belong
here. Style: a single sentence the user could read without context.

At release time the workflow extracts the `## Unreleased` section, uses it
as the App Store "What's New in This Version" copy, then moves it under
a `## v<version> — <date>` heading.

iOS uses the same YYYY.MM.N version scheme as the macOS app and the
website — year.month.count-of-releases-this-month — so the matching
release-tag conventions stay legible across platforms.

Inline markdown (bold, links, code) is not supported by the App Store
listing — keep entries as plain bullet lines.

## Unreleased

- Fixed the Parakeet v3 (multilingual) model download always failing at the end with "model layout looks incomplete" — the downloader was looking for the speech model under an old name and never fetched it. If you were stuck on this error, tap Try again after updating: your previous progress is kept and only the missing piece is downloaded.
- The Dictator keyboard now works as a basic typing keyboard (letters, numbers, and symbols) even when you haven't given it Full Access — instead of showing buttons that can't do anything without it. A slim banner reminds you to turn on Full Access in Settings for dictation, since the keyboard can't reach the microphone on its own. With Full Access on, the Dictate/Assist keyboard is unchanged.
- If you've turned down microphone access, the reminder screen now has an Open Settings button that jumps straight to Dictator's settings, with clearer wording.
- Dictation now records only while Dictator is open. If you switch to another app or lock your phone mid-recording, Dictator finishes transcribing what you've said so far and copies it to the clipboard, ready to paste, instead of the recording carrying on invisibly in the background.
- Dictate and Assist on the main screen now do two things: tap them to toggle recording on, tap them again to stop, OR hold them down for push-to-talk — release stops the recording. Anything shorter than ~0.35 seconds reads as a tap; anything longer reads as a hold. Lets you choose whichever feels right for the moment without changing a setting.
- The Dictator keyboard's Paste row now reads as part of the same family of buttons as the rest of the keys — both the Paste button and the clipboard preview share the same rounded-rectangle shape and height. The preview is also always visible: it shows your last Dictator transcript when that's still on the clipboard, "Content from another app" when something pasteable is there but Dictator can't peek at it without triggering the iOS "Pasted from X" toast, and "Nothing on the clipboard" when there's nothing to paste.
- Assist now hides itself on iPhones that can't run Apple Intelligence (anything older than iPhone 15 Pro). The purple Assist button is gone from the main screen and the Dictator keyboard on those devices, leaving a single full-width Dictate button, and Settings now has an "Assist not available" note that says what's needed. If you have a supported iPhone but Apple Intelligence is turned off, Settings tells you that too and points you to where to turn it on.
- The first-launch walkthrough now has a dedicated step to choose your language model before the download starts — Parakeet English-only (recommended when your phone's language is English, slightly tighter accuracy because the model isn't splitting capacity across other languages) or Parakeet multilingual (covers English plus French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, and other European languages). Both are around 460 MB. The locale-recommended option appears first with a "Recommended" tag, and your choice survives a kill-and-relaunch mid-onboarding so an interrupted download doesn't bounce you back to the picker. After you've picked, the step shows which model you chose and stays tappable so you can swap up to the moment the download starts — once bytes start landing the choice locks in, and from then on you change it in Settings.
- The first-launch model download no longer stops when you switch to another app or lock the phone — it keeps running in the background and resumes from where it left off if your connection drops or you relaunch Dictator. The progress bar now shows megabytes downloaded, total size, and current transfer rate, with Pause and Cancel buttons if you want to step in, and a clear "you can leave the app" message under the bar. The same rich progress view now also appears directly on the first-launch walkthrough's Download model step, and the cellular-data confirmation alert is wired through the walkthrough — tapping Download on cellular no longer looked like it did nothing. The walkthrough's download step now has an explicit "Start download" button right on the card instead of starting silently when you tapped the row.
- Assist on the Dictator keyboard now works off your clipboard. Copy the text you want to transform first (long-press → Copy, like normal), then tap Assist — Dictator opens with that text and you speak your instruction. The button shows "Copy first" with a clipboard icon and stays disabled until you've copied something. iOS limits keyboard extensions to seeing only a sliding window of the host app's text, so the old "use your selection directly" path was silently dropping the middle of long selections in Mail and other rich-text fields; the clipboard route is the only one that works the same everywhere.
- Replaced the Dictator keyboard's auto-paste with a simpler clipboard handoff. Every successful transcription / assist in the Dictator app now copies the result straight to your system clipboard, and the keyboard has a new always-on Paste chip you tap when you're ready to drop it into your field. Position your cursor or select text first if you want the paste to replace something specific. The Paste chip also shows a short preview and word count of what's about to land, so you can confirm before tapping.
- Dropped the "Send to keyboard" button and the in-app keyboard-mode banner — the auto-copy + always-on Paste handles the handoff, so there's no separate mode for the app to enter. Tapping Dictate or Assist on the keyboard still opens the app and auto-starts recording; leave like any in-app session when you're done.
- Tapping the transcript field no longer summons your keyboard — selection, cursor positioning, and the long-press magnifier all still work. A new keyboard button at the bottom of the transcript card brings up the system keyboard when you actually need to type. Stops Dictator's own keyboard from briefly popping up inside Dictator, and keeps the field tappable for selection without disappearing half the screen behind a keyboard.
- The recording row now shows a live waveform next to the model status chip while you're talking, matching the menu-bar HUD on the Mac.
- Dictating multiple times now builds the transcript up instead of replacing it — each press appends to what's already there, so you can capture a thought in a few passes without losing the earlier ones. A new floating Clear button on the left of the transcript card is the explicit way to start fresh; the Undo button has moved to the right of the card to mirror it.
- Dictation now lands at your cursor or replaces the text you have selected, turning the transcript field into a mini editor for fixing typos by voice.
- Using the Dictator keyboard, dictation no longer auto-sends back as soon as the first transcription finishes. You can dictate more, edit by voice or keyboard, run Assist again, and then tap a new Send to keyboard button when you're ready — the transcript flies back when you switch to your original app. Tapping Send flips the button to a green Sent confirmation with a short hint to switch back to your app, in place of the old modal popup.
- Tapping Dictate from the Dictator keyboard now clears whatever was already in the transcript so you can start fresh. Undo brings it back if you tapped the keyboard's mic by mistake.
- Tapping Assist from the Dictator keyboard with nothing selected now operates on the entire text field, not just the bit before your cursor — so a tweak applied mid-paragraph rewrites the whole thing instead of stranding the trailing text.
- Model download now warns you first if you're on cellular data so a 460 MB download doesn't blindside your monthly cap.
- New first-launch walkthrough that takes you through microphone access, downloading the speech model, installing the Dictator keyboard, and enabling Full Access in one place.
- About screen now credits NVIDIA's Parakeet TDT model alongside the FluidAudio library that runs it.
- About screen now shows a Your usage section with two colour-coded cards: Dictation (transcriptions, average words per transcription, words spoken, words delivered) and Assistant (turns, average words per instruction, instruction words, reply words), counted on-device.
- A third Local LLM card shows approximate total tokens the on-device foundation model has generated across cleanup and assistant turns, with input/output breakdown.
- When more than one device is contributing to a shared stats folder, the LLM card splits into a side-by-side of this device vs. all devices.
- Stats move out of the About screen into their own Settings → Stats page.
- Stats footer now reports the actual number of devices contributing to your totals when more than one is sharing the folder.
- New Settings → Shared folder option. Pick a folder in iCloud Drive (typically iCloud Drive › Documents › Dictator on a Mac with Desktop & Documents syncing on) to share your custom vocabulary and usage stats with the Mac app or another iPhone signed into the same iCloud account. Dictation history and assistant conversations stay on this device only.
- Vocabulary editor moves into its own pushed page from Settings, with an entry-count badge on the link, so the Settings screen stops scrolling past the substitution toggles.
- Spoken-cue toggles (Punctuation, Numbers, Clock times, Currency, Emoji names) move to their own Substitutions page reached from Settings.
