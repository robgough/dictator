# Changelog

User-facing changes to Dictator. Internal changes — refactors, build/CI tweaks,
website updates, dependency bumps that don't change behaviour — don't belong
here. Style: a single sentence the user could read without context.

At release time the workflow extracts the `## Unreleased` section, uses it as
the GitHub Release body and the Sparkle "What's new" panel inside the app, then
moves it under a `## v<version> — <date>` heading.

Inline markdown (bold, links, code) is not supported by the converter — keep
entries as plain bullet lines.

## Unreleased

- Long meetings no longer make the app feel sluggish. The live transcript's word-by-word animation was doing steadily heavier work as the transcript grew, which could leave the whole app — the dictation HUD included — stuttering during a long meeting; it now stays light no matter how long the meeting runs.

## v2026.6.8 — 2026-06-22

- The Modes settings have a new layout: a clearer list that shows what each mode does and how it activates, opening into a roomier full-width editor with switch toggles instead of the old cramped two-column view.
- You can now cancel a recording by clicking the top-right corner of the dictation HUD, as well as by pressing Escape.
- New "Auto" choice for what happens to other audio while you dictate (Settings → General → While dictating): it lowers the system volume when your output device allows it, and falls back to pausing Spotify or Music when it can't — typically on USB or Thunderbolt audio interfaces whose driver owns the volume.
- A mode can now always end a dictation with a single trailing space (a new per-mode option in the mode editor), so you can run dictations back-to-back into a terminal or chat without typing a space between them.
- When dictation or Assistant Mode is triggered by holding a bare Option key, pressing a key combination like ⌥3 (the # on a British keyboard) no longer fires a stray dictation — Dictator recognises it as a normal keyboard shortcut and quietly cancels.
- Dictation no longer freezes on "Connecting microphone" for several seconds before recording starts (worst with external USB mics like the Yeti). The recording overlay's animated indicators were forcing a slow GPU render on every start that could stall the whole app; they've been rebuilt to avoid it, so dictation begins promptly.
- Settings → Input no longer fills up with leftover "Dictator Meeting Tap" entries from meeting recordings, and now forgets any input device it hasn't seen in over a week (unless you've ranked it above System default), so the priority list stays tidy.
- Settings has a fresh look: the row of tabs across the top is now a sidebar down the left, matching the modern macOS System Settings style, and the window can now be resized.
- The live preview in the recording HUD is much calmer: it now fills and scrolls a few lines as you speak, animating each change word by word — new words fade in, corrections cross-fade, and the surrounding text glides to make room — instead of a single line that hard-cut and jumped sideways every time the transcription was refined, which was worst during longer dictations.
- Assistant Mode now shows the same live preview of what you're saying as dictation does, instead of leaving the preview area empty.
- Meetings no longer require one specific model to write notes — any sufficiently capable downloaded MLX model now works. Gemma 4 E4B is still the recommended pick (and shows a gentle quality note when you choose a different capable model); the smallest models stay turned off for meetings because their notes aren't reliable on a full transcript.

## v2026.6.7 — 2026-06-15

- Dictation sound cues no longer briefly dip or interrupt other audio (like YouTube or music) when Dictator launches or plays a cue, which was most noticeable on USB audio interfaces.

## v2026.6.6 — 2026-06-15

- While recording a meeting, each audio source now shows a plain-language capture status — a green check and "Hearing audio" once it's actually picking up sound, an amber "No call audio yet" when the call side stays silent — instead of a bare waveform, and no longer reports a live-but-silent device as heard. The recording controls also scroll now so the Stop button stays reachable however much is on screen.

## v2026.6.5 — 2026-06-13

- A meeting's captured screens now show as a thumbnail strip at the top of its Transcript, each labelled with when it was captured; click one to open it full size in Quick Look. (Fixes a bug where captured screenshots were saved but never appeared in the app afterwards.)
- The meeting's transcript markdown now links to each captured screen at the point it was taken, and a new "Export with screenshots…" option saves a folder with the markdown plus the images so the links work anywhere you open it.
- Captured screenshots now sync between your Macs along with the meeting's notes and transcript (they're small), so a meeting opened on another Mac shows its shared screens too.
- Meetings can now capture shared screens: Dictator keeps still frames of slides and demos shown during a call — only the frames that change, scoped to the meeting window so nothing else on your screen is seen, shown as a filmstrip in the meeting's Details. Needs macOS Screen Recording permission, and the frames stay on your Mac.
- Screen capture can be turned on and off per meeting from the "Shared screen" panel while recording — leave the setting off and flip capture on only for the moments that matter, or turn it on in settings to start every meeting capturing. A "Capture now" button grabs a single frame on demand; if capture is off it takes just that one still and stays off.
- The "Shared screen" panel shows which window is being captured and a large preview of the most recent frame; click it (or any screenshot in a meeting's Details) to open it full size in Quick Look. A Change menu points capture at a different meeting window.
- Two speakers in one meeting can no longer both be matched to the same person — the closest voice wins and the other stays separate, so a similar-sounding stranger can't get folded into someone's record.
- The People editor now shows how many people are stored.
- Duplicate people can now be merged: right-click a person in the People editor to fold them into another — emails and voice samples combine, past meetings carry over, and same-named duplicates are flagged with a warning badge.
- Naming a speaker by hand now adds them to your people list — previously only names detected automatically during processing were learned, so a person you named yourself never showed up there.
- Right-clicking a speaker chip when there is nobody to merge with now shows a disabled explanation instead of doing nothing.
- When two people in your people list share a name, a new speaker with that name is left unlinked instead of silently adding a third record — rename to something distinct (like adding a surname) to link them.
- Dictator now recognises people across meetings by voice: once a speaker has a name, their voice is remembered (on this Mac only), and the same person is matched and named automatically in future meetings — renames carry forward, and calendar attendees fill in email addresses. A People editor in Meetings settings shows everything stored and can forget anyone, voice included; the whole feature is one switch.
- Meetings now know their context: which app hosted the call (Zoom, Teams, a browser…) and — with calendar access, asked once — the matching calendar event, with its title, attendees, companies, and scheduled times shown in the meeting's inspector. A matched event names the meeting directly instead of guessing a title from the transcript, and the scheduled end powers a new coach reminder when you're wrapping up with key points still open. Everything stays on your Mac, and calendar matching can be turned off in Meetings settings.
- Meetings now show live conversation metrics while you record — your share of the talking, your pace, and a heads-up when you've held the floor a while. Computed on-device from the recording itself, kept private to the app (never in notes or exports), and can be turned off in Meetings settings.
- The dictation HUD is now a notch island: a black panel that springs down from the top edge of the screen, merging with the notch on MacBooks and docking flush with the top of external displays.
- During meetings the island shows a small coach strip (elapsed time, talk balance) and surfaces occasional one-line nudges — you're monologuing, interrupting, dominating, or racing — with cooldowns so it informs rather than nags. Right-click to hide it for the meeting, or turn it off in Meetings settings.
- Meetings gain a Key points checklist the coach ticks off live as the conversation covers them — in the recording view, or click the island strip to see it there. Type points in, paste a whole markdown list (bullets are stripped), pull in a ready-made set (Interview, Client call) or one of your own saved sets, or start a pad line with ! to flag one mid-meeting. Flagged points get a reminder nudge until covered or dismissed, and recording itself starts instantly — no setup step.
- Each meeting's record notes how the key points went — covered, missed, or dismissed — alongside the private conversation metrics, and a "Show on island" button brings the coach strip back if you've hidden it.
- The live transcript now appears within a beat of each pause instead of trailing the conversation by half a minute: every phrase shows a first-pass transcription immediately, then visibly corrects itself once the polished version is ready — deleting and retyping like a model rethinking.
- It also streams in word by word with a typing cursor, each word fading in, and the cursor blinking gently between phrases. The dictation preview in the island gets the same treatment, with corrections snapping back and retyping as the recogniser refines its draft. (Honours Reduce Motion.)
- The coach has its own section in Meetings settings with a single switch that turns all of it off — nudges, checklist, metrics, report.
- Fixed the coach counting your speakers' audio as you speaking on open-speaker setups — it no longer claims "you've held the floor" when only the other side has been talking.
- Meetings gain a private Coach tab: your conversation metrics at a glance, the key-points scorecard, and a short, blunt written read on how you handled the meeting — graded against what good looks like for that kind of meeting. It writes itself when you generate the notes (re-run it any time), stays on-device, and never appears in the meeting's notes or exports.

## v2026.6.1 — 2026-06-08

## v2026.6.0 — 2026-06-07

- Meetings now needs the Gemma 4 E4B model — in our testing it's the only one that writes meeting notes you can actually rely on. Until it's your selected model the feature stays off: the Settings → Meetings page tells you exactly what to pick, and the model is tagged "required for Meetings" in Settings → Models.
- The orange microphone indicator no longer stays lit when Dictator isn't listening — it now switches off a few seconds after each dictation, so you can trust it again.
- Dictation no longer gets stuck on "Connecting microphone" — every step of mic startup now has a strict time limit, with a retry and a fall-back to the system microphone. If it still can't start, Dictator gives up with a clear message and your next attempt starts fresh.
- Two new models to choose from in Settings → Models: Gemma 4 E2B and E4B, Google's latest. E2B is a great middle-ground option; E4B is the best-quality model Dictator offers (and the one Meetings uses).
- Dictating just an emoji name ("fire emoji") now pastes the emoji and nothing else — no more occasional stray commentary alongside it.
- Create your own meeting note styles: a new Note styles list in Settings → Meetings lets you define exactly which sections your notes should have using a simple template — ALL-CAPS lines name the sections, the text under each tells the model what belongs there. Built-in styles can be duplicated as a starting point, and custom styles show up everywhere a style can be picked, including auto-detect.
- Tune a single meeting's notes without touching your settings: Re-run ▾ → Tune this run takes a one-off instruction ("focus on the budget discussion", "write it in French") applied on top of the style for that meeting only. The instruction is remembered per meeting, so you can keep tweaking and re-running until the notes are right.
- New meeting pad: type your own notes while you record — the left column now splits into your editable pad on top and the machine's live notes below. Anything you jot is treated as ground truth when the final notes are written: your names, owners, decisions and figures win over the transcript. The pad stays editable after the meeting from its own tab, so you can add a thought and re-run.
- The machine-written notes that build during recording are now called Live notes (previously Quick notes), to tell them apart from the pad.
- Every built-in note style now spells out its sections instead of steering a generic shape: retrospectives get What went well / What didn't go well / What to change, stand-ups get a per-person Updates round, client calls get Client asks and Open questions you still owe them, brainstorms get an Ideas section clustered by direction, talks get Key points, and podcasts get Takeaways. Duplicating any style now shows exactly the sections you'd be editing.
- The meeting transcript page can now show the raw tracks: a new Conversation / Raw tracks switch reveals exactly what each side's speech recognition heard — labelled only "You" and "Other side", before working out who said what, with everything the cleanup removed still visible (struck through, with the reason). Useful for checking whether an odd transcript came from the recognition or the cleanup.
- The Live notes tab no longer disappears when a meeting has none — it now explains why there aren't any (imported recording, live transcript or first-pass switched off, or no notes model set up).
- Recording starts faster and more reliably on USB mics, because Dictator now remembers the mic it used last time instead of looking it up from scratch.
- Team-meeting notes now pull out the practical specifics instead of just listing topics: an Updates section for the round-the-room status, a Learnings section for the new information the team came away with, and an Open questions section for anything left unresolved — alongside the usual discussion, decisions and action items.
- Meetings is now available as an early preview. Record a call from the menu bar and get a transcript of both sides — split by speaker — plus notes written for you when it ends, all on-device. It's off by default while we're still shaking it out: turn it on from the new toggle at the top of Settings → Meetings, where a preview notice explains what to expect. While it's off, the meetings entries stay out of the menu bar and the speaker-identification model options are disabled (a model you've already downloaded can still be removed). Meetings also needs an on-device LLM for its notes — if none is set up, the toggle is disabled and the Meetings settings page explains what to enable.
- Dictation is now context-aware: when a dictation starts, Dictator reads the text around your cursor plus distinctive names and terms from a wider sweep of the document (through the same Accessibility permission it already uses for pasting), and the formatting pass uses them to spell names and terminology the way your document does. Names with accents, like Siobhán, are restored even when speech recognition drops the accent and the name only appears much earlier in the document. Per-mode toggle under Context awareness, on by default. Everything stays on your Mac — the context only ever goes to the local model and is never stored — and password fields are never read.
- Dictating into the middle of a sentence now joins cleanly: the pasted text picks up a leading space when it would otherwise glue onto a word, drops the automatic capital letter and trailing full stop when the surrounding sentence carries on, and no longer adds a double space when one already follows the cursor.
- Meeting recordings take a fraction of the disk space they used to. Audio is captured at half the previous size, then automatically re-encoded to AAC once transcription finishes — a one-hour meeting now ends up around 80 MB on disk instead of 1.4 GB, with no audible difference for speech. Meetings you've already recorded are shrunk the same way in the background.
- Long meetings stay responsive: the live transcript and level meters no longer get heavier as a call goes on, so an hours-long recording — and the rest of the app — keeps moving smoothly instead of gradually slowing down.
- Recording one or two long meetings no longer leaves the app sluggish or a later dictation stuck on "Transcribing…": meeting transcription now runs separately from dictation so the two can't block each other, and the speech and speaker models a meeting loads are released once it has finished processing.
- New "Show a live transcript while recording" setting (Meetings, on by default). Turning it off skips drafting the transcript live as you record — useful on very long calls or to save power — while the full transcript is still produced after the meeting ends. Live notes build on the live transcript, so they need it switched on.
- Mic bleed — the remote side of a call that your microphone picks up from your speakers when you're not wearing headphones — is now cancelled in the audio itself, before transcription, by measuring the actual delay and loudness of the leak and removing only what matches it. Previously bleed leaked in as phantom "Me" speech that polluted the transcript, the notes, and the speaker split. Your own voice is kept even when it's much quieter than the call audio and even when you talk over the other side, and the cancellation switches itself off when there's no bleed to remove (e.g. you're on headphones). Controlled by the existing "drop echoes captured by my microphone" setting.
- Any bleed that still slips through to transcription is dropped afterwards by voice: words on the mic track spoken in a remote participant's voice, while the call was making sound, are discarded in favour of the call's own clean copy — so the other side's words can no longer appear garbled under your name.
- The mic and call recordings are time-aligned before their transcripts merge — the two recordings can start several seconds apart, which previously shuffled duplicated words into sentences and misattributed who said what.
- The transcript page now shows the microphone and call audio side by side on one timeline, with everything the cleanup passes removed still visible — struck-through words for dropped bleed and echoes, shaded bands where bleed was silenced in the audio, and the measurements behind it. Meetings processed before this update keep the old single-column view until re-processed.
- The playback controls now float at the bottom of the meeting page, visible on every tab and at any scroll position, so you can pause or scrub while reading. The line under the playhead is highlighted as it plays, and a locate button jumps the transcript to what you're hearing.
- Playing back a meeting recorded without headphones no longer doubles the remote side into an echo: the mic track is automatically turned down while the call audio is talking, and brought back for your own speech.
- Meeting playback now balances the two sides to roughly the same loudness — your mic recording is usually several times quieter than the call audio, so the louder track is brought down to match.
- The transcript reads like a messaging conversation: your turns sit on the right, everyone else on the left, in speech bubbles tinted with each speaker's colour, drawn close together when people talk at once. A speaker who keeps talking gets one bubble with paragraphs rather than a stack of fragments, and an utterance interrupted by the other side is marked with ellipses where it breaks and resumes.
- Meeting notes now make a point of recording the specific facts and stats stated in the conversation — team sizes, org structure, financial figures, dates, product and company names, metrics — exactly as said, instead of rounding or generalising them away. Interview and client-call notes call this out explicitly (tenure, compensation expectations, budgets, vendors and the like).
- Interview notes get dedicated Company and Candidate sections — the checkable facts about the role, company, funding, comp and process on one side, and the candidate's background, numbers and fit signals on the other — plus an Open questions section for anything left unanswered or worth verifying, and a Quotes section with two or three verbatim lines that capture how the person thinks and talks.
- Final meeting notes are written from the cleaned transcript only: on meetings where mic bleed was removed, the rough live outline (which is captured before any cleanup and can carry that bleed) is no longer fed into the final notes pass.
- Removed words (echo duplicates and mic bleed) are no longer shown in the transcript at all — the clean copy of that speech is already on screen as the other side's bubbles. The removal counts stay in the summary line and the copied tracks text.
- Editing speakers (rename or merge) after notes were written now shows a notice on the Final notes tab prompting a Re-run, since the notes still carry the old names.
- Fixed a phantom button floating in the middle of the meeting page (an invisible keyboard-shortcut control that wasn't so invisible).
- The "audio silenced" markers no longer flood the transcript: only a removal longer than 15 seconds shows a (now subtle) marker; the full list remains in the copied tracks text.
- Right-click a speaker chip to merge it into another speaker — the manual fix for one person being split into two (the merged words also give the speaker more context everywhere).
- Transcripts are no longer shredded into runs of single-word lines: speech is grouped into proper turns, and two people talking at once no longer splinter each other's sentences word by word.
- A two-person call no longer shows a phantom third speaker. Speaker identification now listens to the raw microphone recording (echo removal previously chopped the audio it analysed into fragments, splitting your own voice into two "people"), while transcription still works from the echo-cleaned audio.
- Switching between a meeting's Transcript and Notes tabs is much faster on long meetings, and processing a meeting no longer makes the rest of the app — including the dictation HUD — stutter: the heavy audio analysis and transcript assembly now run entirely off the main thread.
- Re-processing a meeting no longer flashes the previous notes as "ready" for a few seconds before snatching them away to rewrite — the meeting now flows straight from transcription into "Rewriting notes…" with the fresh transcript readable underneath.
- Automatic speaker-name guessing is much better at telling who's who: when someone says a name ("thanks, Rory", "over to you, Sam"), they're almost always addressing the other person, so that name is now attributed to the speaker being addressed rather than to the one talking — only a genuine self-introduction ("I'm Rory") names the person speaking.
- Finished meeting notes now show which conversation style they were written for — the type you picked, or, when left on Auto-detect, the one Dictator worked out from the transcript (shown with a sparkle, like guessed speaker names). Re-run ▾ still lets you override it.
- The live notes taken while a meeting is recording can now correct themselves as the conversation moves on — if a decision is reversed or a number is restated later, the relevant point is revised or dropped rather than left stale. (New toggle under Meetings settings, "Correct live notes as the conversation moves on"; on by default.)
- Meetings now tell apart the people on a call more reliably — a fix to the speaker-grouping sensitivity that was, in effect, the most aggressive at merging distinct voices, so a two-person call no longer collapses into a single speaker.
- A stray word or two bleeding in from the other side of a call no longer creates a phantom "Me" (or an extra) speaker on a meeting where you were only listening.
- Meeting notes are stricter about who said what: a point that's merely about a person is no longer mis-stated as something that person said, and the final notes now trust the transcript's speaker labels over the rougher live outline captured during the call.
- Speakers in a meeting are now told apart by shape as well as colour — each speaker gets a distinct little symbol next to their name — so they stay distinguishable if you're colour-blind or reading in greyscale.
- The Meetings screens now respect your system text size: labels, chips, and notes scale with the Display & Text Size setting instead of being pinned to fixed sizes.
- Very short recordings get a lighter note — a brief one-to-three-sentence summary, plus action items only if there were any — instead of being padded out with empty Discussion and Decisions sections.
- A consistent visual style across the meeting chips and pills (speaker, draft, speaker-count, owners), so they read as one set rather than slightly different one-offs.

- Your meeting notes and transcripts now sync between your Macs. They're written to your synced Dictator folder (the same one as settings, vocabulary, and history — by default ~/Documents/Dictator), so a meeting recorded on one Mac can be read on another; switching back to the Meetings window re-scans, so meetings from another Mac appear. The large audio recordings stay on the Mac that recorded them and are never uploaded — you'll see the notes and transcript everywhere, with audio playback on the recording Mac. The Meetings window explains this: a line at the foot of the list, and — when you open a meeting whose audio lives on another Mac — a note in place of the play bar telling you where the recording is. (If a previous build had started syncing the audio, this version moves it back out of the synced folder for you.)
- Meetings now try to work out who was speaking. After a recording is processed, Dictator reads the conversation and replaces the generic "Speaker 1 / Speaker 2" labels with real names when someone is clearly introduced or addressed (for example "thanks, Rory") — so the notes attribute points and action items to people by name. Guessed names show a small sparkle so you can spot them at a glance, and you can correct any name; a name you type by hand is never overwritten.
- The assistant hotkey now works on the meeting you're looking at. With the Meetings window focused, hold (or tap) the assistant hotkey and speak a question or an edit about the open notes — the assistant dialog opens, listens, and either answers ("what did we decide about pricing?") or previews a rewrite you can apply ("group the action items by owner"). It's the same on-device assistant as dictation, with the whole notes as context. Finished notes also get a prominent Assistant button, and a "Hold ⌘⌥A to ask" hint appears while the window is focused.
- New setting, on by default: a quick tap of the dictation or assistant hotkey now starts recording and latches it on — tap again to stop — while holding the key down past a moment still works as classic push-to-talk. Matches the iOS app. Turn it off under Settings → Hotkey behaviour if you only want hold-to-talk.
- Meetings now require the Parakeet speech model, which they always use to transcribe on-device. If it isn't downloaded yet, recording or importing a meeting prompts you to download it first (and opens Settings → Models so you can watch progress) instead of stalling partway through a recording.
- Fixed messy meeting notes rendering: timestamps the model copied from the transcript (e.g. "[Rory Sutherland · 0:21]") now become a clean clickable time pill instead of raw text, bold topic lead-ins in the Discussion no longer get mistaken for speaker chips, and sections the model wrapped in code fences render as proper notes instead of grey monospace. Existing notes pick this up without re-running.
- New meeting notes style, "Conversation / podcast", for when you're listening in on a discussion between other people rather than taking part — it summarises the substance and what each speaker said, and doesn't try to pin action items on you. A listen-only recording (no "Me" in it) left on Auto-detect now uses this style automatically.
- The notes-style steering for every meeting type (stand-up, retro, 1-on-1, client call, …) was rewritten to match the markdown notes format, so picking a style now shapes the notes correctly instead of fighting it.
- The rough notes captured live during a meeting are now kept after the full notes are written, as a "Quick notes" tab alongside Final notes and Transcript — the live pass accumulates as the meeting runs and is often more complete than the polished rewrite. The final pass is also less prone to over-shortening a long meeting: it's told to stay thorough and is given the live outline as a checklist so it doesn't drop points.
- Meeting transcripts (live and final) now run through your dictation Vocabulary — the same dictionary of names, jargon, and preferred spellings — so those land correctly in the transcript and the notes.
- Live meeting transcription is much cleaner. It segments on natural pauses, and rather than committing each phrase immediately it holds the last couple back and re-transcribes them together with the speech that follows — so a thought continued after a pause is no longer punctuated as if the sentence had ended. Only settled text feeds the notes (the live transcript trails the speaker by a couple of phrases as a result), and when you stop the recording the held-back tail is finalised and added in, so the quick notes are complete.
- Stopping a recording now works on the first click even when the Meetings window wasn't focused — previously the first click only brought the window forward and you had to click Stop again. Notes bullets can carry a timestamp; click it to jump the transcript and audio to that moment and check what was actually said. You can also browse your other meetings while one is still recording (a "Recording" banner up top takes you back), and ⌘1/⌘2 switch between Notes and Transcript.
- The finished meeting notes now have an Assistant button next to Edit: ask a question about the notes ("what did we decide about pricing?") and get an answer, or tell it how to change them ("tighten the action items", "group these by owner") and preview the rewrite before replacing the notes. You can type the request or tap the mic and speak it. It runs the same on-device assistant as dictation, with the whole notes as context.
- A meeting's three views are now Transcript, Quick notes (the rough notes captured live), and Final notes. The Copy button copies — and is labelled for — whichever you're looking at.
- The Meetings experience got a big polish pass. While recording, the notes are now the focus — a two-column screen with the notes building on the left and the recording controls, level meters, and live transcript on the right. New notes points animate in and briefly highlight as they're captured, there's a running "3 topics · 8 points · updated 12s ago" status so you can see it's working, and the meeting type (stand-up, retro, 1-on-1…) can be set during the call so the final notes come out in the right shape. The level meters are now honest and show a green check once each side is being heard, and the menu-bar icon turns into a red record dot while a meeting is recording so you always know it's on. When processing finishes in the background you get a "notes ready" notification. Action items render as checkable to-dos with the owner shown as a colour-coded chip, and finished notes carry a "written by <model> · time" line.
